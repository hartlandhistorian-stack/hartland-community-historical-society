        100th Anniversary of the Hartland Covered Bridge (1901-2001)
1. The Anniversary Committee that organized the celebration for the Hartland Bridge 100th year included the former mayor Betty Lou Craig, Rev. Maynard Rector, town clerk Judy Dee, Co-chair John Glass, Shirley Green, and Co-chair Doris E Kennedy. Dressed in period 
2. On May 13, 2001, local doctors Ghanem and Stocek, accompanied by their family, reenacted the unofficial opening of the bridge, a historical event prompted by a medical emergency that occurred the day before the official opening to the public in 1901.
3. Premier Bernard Lord and his children, Sebastian and Jasmine, cut the ribbon to mark the start of the next 100 years for the Hartland Bridge. From left to right, those participating were Miss Hartland 2000 Crystal Mean, committee members Shirley Green and Rev. Maynard Rector, committee co-chair Doris E. Kennedy, Premier Lord, Hartland Town Clerk Judy Dee, Miss New Brunswick 2000 Heather Harris, and committee member and former mayor Betty Lou Craig.
4. Barney DeLong drives the Hartland Fire Department’s antique fire truck during a parade that reenacts a procession of dignitaries.
5. Town Crier Leonard Orser welcomes everyone to the historical event, assisted by Doris Kennedy.
6. Eugene Allison takes part in the ceremonial parade of dignitaries as a representative of the famous Hartland International Salmon Pool.
7.  At the end of the day, fireworks light up the sky above the bridge, followed by a dance on the bridge ramp.
8. Hugh Clark participated in the parade of dignitaries, showcasing his wireless emergency transmission radio, which had transmitted signals across the Atlantic since December 1901.
9. The Imperial Order of the Daughters of the Empire, founded in January 1900 and established in Hartland in 1924, were represented in a horse-drawn carriage owned by Judy and Dale Manuel.
10. Hartland Historians and 100th Anniversary co-chairs, John Glass and Doris E. Kennedy.
11. The 100th Anniversary dinner was graced by the presence of New Brunswick Lt. Governor Marilyn Trenholme Counsell, the evening’s special guest and dignitary.
12. Premier Bernard Lord, accompanied by his wife, Diane Lord, their daughter Jasmine, and son Sebastin, is seated at the anniversary dinner at the United Baptist Church. Observing are Co-Chair John Glass and Deputy Premier Dale Graham.
13. Premier Bernard Lord at the 100th Anniversary Ribbon Cutting.
14. During the parade of dignitaries, RCMP members showcased the evolution of their uniforms, beginning with the white helmet worn at Queen Victoria’s Jubilee in 1897 through to the broad-brimmed Stetson hat, the transition from close-fitting yellow-striped blue breeches to jodhpurs, and various changes in belts, boots, gloves, and other accessories.
15. Dinner in the United Baptist Church consisted of the reenactment of the official opening of the bridge on July 4, 1901.
16. On Sunday, May 13, 2001, large crowds gathered to witness a re-enactment of the historic medical emergency crossing from May 13, 1901. The event featured a horse-drawn carriage, owned by Judy and Dale Manual, carrying present-day Hartland doctors Ghanem and Stocek and their three sons: Noah, Matthew, and Alex.
17. During the parade of dignitaries, members of the 100th Anniversary Committee, dressed in period costumes, ride in a buckboard wagon representing the 1901 Hartland Bridge Company, the builders of the bridge.
18. To celebrate the bridge’s anniversary, Weldon Matthews wrote a play for the library board depicting the Hartland Delegation’s appeal to the government for a bridge in Hartland on March 15, 1898—an effort that ultimately proved unsuccessful. This was performed at the anniversary dinner.  Standing (left to right) Leonard Orser, Alton Shaw, Dalton Ellis, John Glass, Mark Atwater, Barb Shaw and Ann Ellis. Sitting Aage & Bea Skaarup (off stage) Elta Rideout, Ralph Shaw and Ruth Sawler.
19. Weldon Matthews’ play, The Hartland Delegation, premiered at the library dinner on April 7, 2001. It was so well-received that it was performed again at the anniversary dinner on July 4, 2001. Standing (left to right): Leonard Orser, Alton Shaw, Dalton Ellis, Mark Atwater, John Glass, and Ann Ellis. Seated: Elta Rideout and Ralph Shaw. Hidden are Ruth Sawler and Barb Shaw. Musical accompaniment by Aage & Bea Skaarup.
20. The ladies' singing group Muse performed at both the ribbon-cutting ceremony and the dinner on July 4, 2001. From left to right: Beth McMillan, Carolyn Everett, Jeanne Shaw, Judy Bouma, Sandy Langdon, Joanne Barrett, Rachel MacDonald, and Karen Arnold.
