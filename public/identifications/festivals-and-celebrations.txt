13. On February 15, 1965, the new single red maple leaf flag was raised at the town hall by Mayor Arden Nevers. This replaced the Canadian Red ensign, which included the Union Jack; many veterans opposed it because they had fought under it. The Hartland High School students marched down from the school to observe this memorial event.
14. 1965 Charlotte Anne Dunffield’s Carleton County Corps Majorettes at Hartland at High School. 
15. In 1967, the Sisters of Hartland from Edward Laskey Temple No. 29 included a talented group of performers known as “The Humdingers.” In addition to entertaining, The Humdingers compiled a cookbook of time-tested traditional recipes. They are pictured riding a float in the Hartland Potato Festival parade.
16. The Shriners Caboose Unit raises funds for children's hospitals. Pictured here are Cecil Shaw on the left and Harry Lanyon standing on the step to the right. The Shriners' caboose was always a highlight, eagerly anticipated in every parade.
17. In 1989, Fred Hatfield served as the parade marshal during the Covered Bridge Spud and Spoke Days. The Hartland Potato Festival received its new name after a contest was held among Hartland school students. Tyson Craig earned a $50 prize for his winning suggestion.
18. The 2018 Little Miss Pageant was held at the Hartland High School for Hartland’s Homecoming during the 100th Anniversary of the incorporation of the Town of Hartland.
19. July 1, 1927, Confederation Parade of the 60th Diamond Jubilee.
