	•	The Assembly of God, affiliated with the Pentecostal denomination, was dedicated on July 6, 1956. Eight charter members founded the church and built it on land purchased from G. R. Miller on Main Street. The church was enlarged and its interior improved in 1966. Pastors John and Norma Write served the congregation. (From K. Hatfield’s History of Churches)
	•	Parsonage of the Free Baptist Church, next to the Free (Christian) Baptist Church and the Hartland Superior School on lower Main Street. The parsonage was vacated and rented to Estey and Curtis for their pharmacist, George Dunham. It was later sold to Harold Aiton, then Adrian Hovey and Sterling Hallett. This was built in the late 1800s for the Free Baptist Church and remains in use today. (2026)
	•	The Free (Christian) Baptist Church was organized in 1843 by Rev. G. W. Orser. Construction of the church began in 1871 using 10 x 10 timbers and no nails, but work stopped due to a doctrinal split. Rev. Charles McMullin continued the congregation with just 2 of the original 38 members, and the church was finally dedicated in 1875. Another split occurred in 1905, after which the congregation joined the Calvinist Church, forming the United Baptist Church. The building was later sold to Scott Sipprelle, who converted it into a garage with living quarters upstairs. It subsequently served as a blacksmith shop and was later sold to house the Hartland Furniture Company. This historic building, the oldest church in the area, was demolished in 1974.
	•	In 1912-13, under the ministries of Rev. H. S. Dow and Rev. P. J. Trafton, the Reformed Baptist Church began work on a cemetery. The land at the end of High Street, donated by G Burtt, W. P. MacMullin, and J Bradley, was used for this purpose. The church maintained the cemetery until 1922, with George Grey serving as caretaker.
	•	The Holy Trinity Anglican Church stands prominently at the corner of Orser and High Street. For four years, Rev. Franchetti conducted services in public halls until, on June 18, 1917, the church acquired land. As a talented architect, he designed the striking Gothic-Norman-style cement-block building, using materials supplied by the Hartland Cement Company. 
	•	In 1924, the Hartland Cement Company began constructing the massive, steeply pitched gable roof of the Holy Trinity Anglican Church. Distinctive pointed arch recessed windows, later adorned with stained glass, added to the church's charm. Thanks to numerous volunteers, the building was completed and dedicated in 1925.
	•	The interior of the Anglican Church is accessed through the narthex at the base of the tower and belfry, leading into a spacious nave. The nave features wainscoting along the perimeter and decorative panel framing, rising from the wainscoting to the roof, accented by rich oak moldings. A central aisle runs toward the east wall, flanked by butternut pews on either side. The chancel, raised three steps above the nave, provides seating for the choir and houses the altar. Large, stained-glass, dedicated pointed-arch windows adorn both the east and west walls.
	•	This photo shows the interior of the Methodist Church, which later became part of the United Church of Canada, decorated for Thanksgiving. It was taken after 1913, when the church was lifted to add a basement with concrete walls and floor, allowing for the installation of a furnace and the construction of classrooms.
	•	The first service of the Methodist Church, later known as the United Church, was held on November 17, 1873, in Matheson Hall on Upper Main Street. As the congregation grew, a new church was constructed in 1894 under the leadership of Rev. H. S. Young. The cornerstone was laid on July 11, and the church was dedicated on October 7. Today, Trinity United Church of Canada is recognized as a Local Historic Place for its architectural significance and its lasting religious and cultural impact.

	•	The interior of the United Church, completed in 1894, reflects a classic Roman church layout. Its 26-by-45-foot floor plan features a west-facing narthex just inside the double doors, leading into the nave. Unusually, the central aisle of the nave slopes downward from the narthex to the chancel, a rare architectural feature in churches of this kind. Ash wainscoting and pews add warmth, while the ceiling is finished in varnished spruce. The church’s prominence is highlighted by a tower above the narthex, crowned with a steeple and belfry.

	•	The Upper Brighton Primitive Baptist Church was founded in 1874 by Rev. G. W. Orser, who previously led a 2,000-member group known as the ‘Orserites’, later called the Primitive Baptists. Hartland is one of the few communities recognized as the birthplace of this Christian denomination. The Tabernacle, located on the west side of the St. John River, hosted annual gatherings where a monument stands in honor of Rev. G. W. Orser, founder of the Primitive Baptist denomination. This picture was taken during their quarterly meeting fellowship with Holmesville, Fredericton, Bristol and Upper Brighton, which they still do today.

	•	The Reformed Baptist Church was organized on December 3, 1888, and initially held meetings in Matheson Hall. In the spring of 1892, construction began on their own church building, which was dedicated on November 6, 1892, under the ministry of Rev. S. A. Baker. The congregation was motivated to build their own space because the remnants of the 'Wild West Show' left in Matheson Hall on Saturday nights made it unsuitable for Sunday worship.

	•	The church was rededicated on January 4, 1914, after a 20 x 23-foot addition was built at the rear. Members of the Wesleyan Church (formerly Reformed Baptist Church) held their final service in the Hartland church on September 16, 1984. Starting Wednesday, September 19, 1984, all services moved to the new location in Somerville.
	•	This photograph of the Reformed Baptists Sabbath School was taken in September 1896. The original image of Anna Boyer was donated to the history collection by Belle Swim.
	•	In April 1950, a full basement was added beneath the rest of the church, expanding on the earlier partial basement that housed the heating system. This addition provided much-needed space for classrooms as the Sabbath School continued to grow. Pictured from left to right are: Burpee Dow, Miles Veysey, Elias Crabbe, Charles Orser, Willis Brown, Gene Dow, Arthur Bryant, Fred Smith, Gilbert Brown, Carles Shaw, and one unidentified individual.
	•	On May 3, 1895, the Baptist Church was organized. Later, at a joint meeting on July 1, 1906, the Free Baptist Church and the Baptist Church united to form a single congregation, with Rev. A. T. Baker serving as their first pastor. The church building, constructed in 1898 for $2200, originally served the Baptist Church before the union. This photograph was taken after 1901.
	•	This photograph shows the new pipe organ installed in the United Baptist Church at a cost of $2,900, with $2,620 in donations pledged toward its purchase 2,620. The tonal section of the organ was placed in the corner of the choir loft, south of the memorial window, while the console was positioned at the centre of the choir loft, behind the pulpit. Casavant organ was dedicated on February 18, 1945. Mass chimes donated by Mrs. McGee in 1951.
	•	Renovations to the United Baptist Church took place in 1910, and a second annex was added and dedicated on December 17, 1922. For many years, the church bell rang out an invitation to worship each Sunday until it was silenced on October 16, 1963, when a fire destroyed the church, resulting in a loss of $100,000. The bell was salvaged and now sits on the front lawn of the Baptist Church on Main Street south of the bridge.
	•	The United Baptist Church was rebuilt and dedicated on December 6, 1964.
	•	The final service at the old Wesleyan Church took place on September 16, 1984. Beginning September 19, 1984, all services were held in the new building in Somerville.
