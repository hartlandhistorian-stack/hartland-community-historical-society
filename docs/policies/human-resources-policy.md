# Hartland Community Historical Society — Human Resources Policy

**Adopted by:** Hartland Community Historical Society (the "Society")
**Applies to:** Board members, staff (paid or contracted), and volunteers
**Effective date:** _[to be set by the Board]_
**Review cycle:** Every 2 years, or sooner if legislation or operations change

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

The Hartland Community Historical Society currently operates without a physical
museum building and without paid staff — its work is carried out by its
founder, its Board, and community volunteers. This policy exists to:

- Set clear, fair standards for everyone who does work on the Society's
  behalf, whether unpaid or paid, occasional or ongoing.
- Protect the people who give their time — many of them seniors and
  long-time community members — and protect the collection they are
  entrusted with.
- Give the Society a ready framework to grow into: if the Society hires its
  first paid employee, opens a physical museum space, or takes on
  contractors, the same policy scales rather than needing to be invented
  from scratch under time pressure.

This policy applies to:
1. **Board members** — governance volunteers with fiduciary duties.
2. **Volunteers** — anyone contributing time without pay: photo scanning,
   research, identification, poster installation, event support, website
   contributions, etc.
3. **Staff** — any paid employee or contractor, present or future.

Where a section applies only to one group, it says so explicitly. Where it
doesn't, it applies to everyone.

---

## 2. Guiding principles

- **Equal opportunity.** The Society does not discriminate in recruitment,
  role assignment, or treatment on the basis of race, colour, ancestry,
  place of origin, religion, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity
  or expression, marital or family status, disability, or any other ground
  protected under applicable New Brunswick and Canadian human rights
  legislation.
- **Respect for community and subjects.** Much of the Society's collection
  depicts identifiable living and deceased community members. Everyone
  working with the collection is expected to treat subjects, donors, and
  their families with the same respect owed to any colleague.
- **Proportionality.** Because the Society is small and volunteer-driven,
  this policy is intentionally lightweight where a full HR bureaucracy
  would be a burden, and firm only where the risk warrants it (child
  safety, collections handling, personal information, money).

---

## 3. Roles and structure

- The **Board** sets policy, holds fiduciary and legal responsibility for
  the Society, and appoints or removes anyone in a coordinating role.
- A **Volunteer Coordinator** (a Board member or delegate) is responsible
  for day-to-day recruitment, onboarding, scheduling, and support of
  volunteers.
- **Staff**, if and when the Society employs anyone, report to the Board
  or to a designated supervisor named in their offer letter.

Every active volunteer or staff member should know who their point of
contact is for questions, concerns, or scheduling. If that is unclear,
default to the Volunteer Coordinator or Board Chair.

---

## 4. Recruitment and onboarding

### 4.1 Volunteers
- Anyone may express interest via the Society's contact channel
  (currently nursekennedy@hotmail.com or the site's Contribute/Contact
  pages).
- A short informal conversation establishes interests, availability, and
  any role requiring extra screening (see 4.3).
- New volunteers receive:
  - A walkthrough of how the collection and website are organized (see
    the project `README.md`).
  - The relevant sections of this policy — at minimum Sections 5, 6, and
    9.
  - Contact information for their Volunteer Coordinator.
- There is no minimum time commitment. Volunteers may do as little as
  submit one corrected caption or as much as manage an entire category.

### 4.2 Staff
- Any paid position is approved by the Board before recruitment begins,
  with a written job description covering duties, hours, pay, and
  reporting line.
- Postings are open and public where practical; hiring decisions are made
  by the Board or its delegate.
- New staff receive a written offer letter, a copy of this policy, and a
  probationary review point at 3 months.

### 4.3 Screening for higher-trust roles
Roles with access to any of the following require a reference check and,
where the role involves regular contact with minors or vulnerable
persons, a criminal record check (at the Society's expense):
- Financial accounts or donations.
- Personal information about living community members (addresses,
  family details, health information mentioned in identifications).
- Unsupervised work with minors (e.g., a future school-visit program).

---

## 5. Code of conduct

Everyone working on the Society's behalf agrees to:
- Treat fellow volunteers, staff, board members, donors, and members of
  the public with courtesy and respect.
- Represent the Society accurately and avoid speaking on its behalf
  publicly (press, social media, official correspondence) unless
  authorized to do so.
- Avoid conflicts of interest — e.g., using the collection or donor
  relationships for personal financial gain — and disclose any potential
  conflict to the Board as soon as it arises.
- Never accept gifts, payment, or favours in exchange for altering,
  withholding, or prioritizing handling of collection material.
- Follow the collections-handling practices in Section 7 when working
  with physical photographs, posters, or original documents.
- Comply with copyright and rights information already recorded for each
  item (see the `credit` and `rights` fields in `content/photos/*.json`)
  and not represent a photograph as freely usable if it is marked
  otherwise.

Breach of this code is handled under Section 11 (Concerns, complaints,
and discipline).

---

## 6. Confidentiality and privacy

The collection contains identifiable images of, and information about,
private individuals, including minors in some historical photographs and
family details shared by donors in confidence.

- Do not share unpublished donor contact information, family details, or
  sensitive personal history learned through Society work, except as
  needed to do the work itself.
- Any request to remove or restrict a photograph (e.g., from a living
  subject or their family) is routed to the Volunteer Coordinator or
  Board promptly and handled respectfully — do not argue the request or
  publish it further while it is under review.
- Financial and donor-giving records, if any exist, are accessible only
  to the Treasurer and Board.

---

## 7. Collections handling and site content standards

- Original photographs, posters, and identification documents are
  irreplaceable. Handle them with clean, dry hands; avoid food, drink,
  and direct sunlight near originals; use archival sleeves/folders where
  provided.
- Digitize at the resolution and format described in `README.md` before
  any physical original is returned to a donor or filed away.
- When adding or editing a `content/photos/*.json` entry, do not alter or
  delete an existing identification (`description`, `people`, `date`)
  without a documented reason — Doris Kennedy's original identifications
  are historical record, not just captions. Corrections are welcome;
  silent overwrites are not.
- Never publish a photograph, identification, or poster the Society does
  not have the rights or permission to publish (see `credit`/`rights`
  fields).

---

## 8. Health and safety

- No one should work alone in situations involving physical risk (e.g.,
  moving heavy poster boards, working from a ladder to install a wall
  poster). Arrange a second person or Board sign-off for such tasks.
- Report any injury, near-miss, or unsafe condition (at the library, at a
  storage location, or at an event) to the Volunteer Coordinator or Board
  as soon as practical.
- If the Society opens a physical museum space in future, this section
  will be expanded to cover public-facing premises safety, emergency
  procedures, and accessibility of the space itself.

---

## 9. Protection of minors and vulnerable persons

Applies to any current or future activity where volunteers or staff
interact directly with children or vulnerable adults (school visits,
public events, oral-history interviews with elderly community members).

- No one-on-one unsupervised contact with a minor; a second adult must be
  present or reachable.
- Anyone in such a role must have completed the screening in Section 4.3.
- Any disclosure or suspicion of abuse or neglect is reported immediately
  to the appropriate authorities and to the Board Chair — this
  obligation is not waived by any other section of this policy.

---

## 10. Recognition and support

- Volunteer contributions (photo counts, research, events) are
  acknowledged periodically — e.g., in the Society's communications or an
  annual thank-you.
- Volunteers may request a letter confirming their contribution (useful
  for school, work, or immigration purposes).
- The Society will make reasonable accommodations for a volunteer's or
  employee's disability, health condition, or other need, consistent with
  applicable human rights law.

---

## 11. Concerns, complaints, and discipline

- **Raising a concern:** Speak to the Volunteer Coordinator or any Board
  member. Concerns about a Board member go to the Board Chair or, if the
  concern is about the Chair, to another Board member.
- **Handling a concern:** The recipient acknowledges it within a
  reasonable time, looks into it fairly, and keeps it as confidential as
  the situation allows.
- **Response to a substantiated breach of this policy**, in rough order
  of severity:
  1. A private conversation and reminder of expectations.
  2. A written warning from the Board.
  3. Suspension from Society activities while the matter is resolved.
  4. Removal as a volunteer, termination of employment, or removal from
     the Board (the latter following the Society's bylaws).
  Serious matters (theft, harassment, danger to a minor, financial
  misconduct) skip directly to suspension pending investigation, and are
  reported to police or other authorities where the law requires it.
- No one faces retaliation for raising a good-faith concern.

---

## 12. Time off, hours, and pay (staff only)

Applies once the Society has paid staff; not applicable to volunteers,
who set their own availability.

- Hours, pay rate, and any overtime or leave entitlements are set out in
  the employee's offer letter and comply with New Brunswick's
  Employment Standards Act at minimum.
- Leave requests are made to the employee's supervisor with reasonable
  notice, except in emergencies.

---

## 13. Ending a working relationship

- **Volunteers** may step back at any time, with or without notice; a
  courtesy heads-up to the Volunteer Coordinator is appreciated so work
  can be handed off.
- **Staff** resignations follow the notice period in their offer letter
  or applicable law, whichever is longer.
- On departure from any higher-trust role (Section 4.3), access to
  accounts, shared drives, donation records, and any physical keys is
  revoked promptly.
- The Society will provide a reference or letter of contribution on
  request for anyone who leaves in good standing.

---

## 14. Policy administration

- This policy is approved and amended by the Board.
- Questions about interpretation go to the Board Chair.
- This document lives at `docs/policies/human-resources-policy.md` in the
  Society's website repository so it stays versioned alongside the rest
  of the Society's records; substantive changes should be made via a
  reviewed update, not silently.
